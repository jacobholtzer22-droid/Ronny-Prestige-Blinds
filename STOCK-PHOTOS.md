# Stock photos

**Three, all in the homepage hero. Nowhere else on the site.**

Every other image is the client's own work, crew, or installed product, pulled
from the client's current website at prestigewoodblinds.com, which the client
owns and publishes.

## The three hero slides

The hero is the only full-bleed image on the site: on a 1920 screen at
devicePixelRatio 2 it needs 3840 device pixels across. The client's own
photographs are 1024 to 1290 pixel copies off his WordPress site, which the
pipeline could only emit at 1024w, a 3.75x upscale that was visibly soft. These
three are licensed stock at 3200px wide, landscape, each showing one of the
products. They are placeholders: swap them back out the moment the client sends
original job photos. See CLIENT-TODO.md, first item.

Every real photograph they replaced is still on the site, on its product tile,
in the gallery and on About. None was removed.

All three were viewed before use and carry no faces, no children, no logos, no
brand names and no watermarks. Their alt text is deliberately generic and never
names the business or a city, because these are not the client's jobs.

One candidate was rejected at this stage on the photographer rule: Pexels 6764827
is a good roller-shade interior, but the photographer's profile is a Dutch window
blinds retailer (rolgordijnenexpert.nl), so a competitor's marketing photograph
would have been running as this client's hero.

## Everything else

The build prompt allowed up to eight licensed stock placeholders for the hero
and for any service page without a real photo of that product. Only the hero
needed them: all six services are covered by real photographs. See PHOTO-MAP in
the build report for the source URL of every file.

The old site also carried stock and manufacturer catalog imagery, including
123RF-style numeric filenames (`149791943_m.jpg`, `150118400_m.jpg`,
`168038322_m.jpg`, `190344603_m.jpg`), generic descriptive filenames
(`automated-roller-blinds-in-modern-office.jpg`,
`beautiful-interior-with-installed-electric-blinds.jpg`,
`looking-through-the-window-.jpg`, `man-installing-blackout-blinds.png`), a 3D
render (`vertical-blinds.jpg`), and a set of catalog-styled product shots. None
of it was carried over: the old site's stock license does not transfer with the
images, and there is no evidence one was ever bought.
