## What styles of blinds are there?

Horizontal blinds are the familiar ones: slats that tilt to set how much light comes in, in wood for warmth, metal for a slimmer profile, or vinyl where there is moisture. Vertical blinds run the slats the other way and stack to one side, which is why they are the usual choice for a sliding glass door or a wide window. Roman blinds fold into soft pleats as they rise, for a window you want to read as fabric rather than as a mechanism.

## Can you match what is already in the room?

Yes. Blinds are custom made from a wide range of materials, colors, and finishes, and the color scheme in the room can be matched rather than approximated. Where nothing standard fits the opening or the look, special orders and custom options are available. We bring samples to the consultation so you are choosing against your own wall color and flooring, not against a screen.

## What goes on a sliding glass door?

Vertical blinds are the straightforward answer, because the slats stack clear of the doorway. Panel track shutters and wide zebra shades also cover that opening well and read quite differently, so we will usually show you all three on the same visit and let the room decide. A slider is the most common window in a house here, and it is worth getting right.

## Can blinds be motorized?

Yes. Blinds can take a motor and run from a remote, a wall switch, or a phone app. It is most worth doing on a high window, behind furniture, or on any blind you are adjusting several times a day as the sun moves round.
