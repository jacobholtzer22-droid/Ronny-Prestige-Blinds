## What is a zebra shade?

A zebra shade carries alternating bands of sheer and solid fabric on one shade. Line the bands up and the solid sections overlap, so the shade reads as solid and the room is private. Offset them and the sheer sections line up instead, so light filters in and you keep the view out. They are also called transitional shades, because one shade does both jobs rather than two.

## Do they filter light or darken the room?

Both, and that is the point of them. The same shade carries light filtering and room darkening fabric, so how much light comes in is set by how far you move the bands. Through the day you leave them offset for the view and soft light. In the evening you close them over for privacy. In between, you can shield the room from the worst of the glare without shutting it down.

## Where do they work best?

Living rooms, bedrooms, and studies are where most of ours go, because those are the rooms where you want the view through the day and privacy at night. They sit particularly well in a contemporary room, where the clean horizontal banding suits the lines. They are built to the opening, so a wide span or a sliding glass door is a normal job rather than a special one.

## Can zebra shades be motorized?

Yes. Zebra shades are available motorized and run from a remote, a wall switch, or a phone app, and they can be added to a smart home system. We fit and program the motor as part of the installation. On a tall window or a wide slider it is usually worth doing, because those are exactly the ones you would otherwise have to stretch to reach.
