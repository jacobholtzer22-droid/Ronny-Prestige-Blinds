## Which material should I choose?

Wood is the classic. It has the warmth and the grain, and it is what most people picture when they picture shutters. Vinyl and composite are the practical answers for a bathroom, a kitchen, or any window that takes direct sun and humidity, because they hold their shape and wipe clean. All three come in a range of colors, and we can match the color scheme already in the room.

## Traditional tilt or hidden tilt?

A traditional shutter has a center tilt rod down each panel. You move the rod, the louvers follow. A hidden tilt shutter moves the louvers from a mechanism inside the panel, so the face of the shutter stays clean and the louver spacing stays even. Hidden tilt reads more modern; traditional reads more classic. Both do the same job on light and privacy.

## What goes on a sliding glass door?

Panel track shutters. Rather than hinged panels swinging into the room, broad panels slide along a track, so they can cover a wide opening without crowding it and stack to one side when you want the view back. They suit sliding doors, french doors, and the wide spans that are common in houses here.

## How does the installation go?

We measure the windows, you choose the style, material, and finish, and the shutters are made to those measurements. On installation day we take down whatever is there now, prepare the frames, and mount the shutters either to the window trim or inside the recess, depending on the window. Then we adjust everything, check each panel opens and closes properly, and clean up before we leave.

## What does it cost and how long does it take?

Shutters are made to order, so both answers depend on the size of the order and the options you pick. You get an estimate from the free in-home consultation, and we offer same-day estimates. The schedule is set at that same visit, once the windows are measured and the style is settled.
