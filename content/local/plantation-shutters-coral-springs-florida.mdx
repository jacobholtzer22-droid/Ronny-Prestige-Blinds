## What a plantation shutter is

Wide louvers mounted in a frame, fitted to the opening rather than hung in front of it. You tilt the louvers to set light and privacy, and because the whole thing is a built unit rather than a hanging treatment, it reads as part of the house. That is the reason shutters outlast fashions: they look like joinery, not like window dressing.

## Choosing the material here

Wood is the classic, with the warmth and the grain, and in a dry, shaded room it is the nicest thing you can fit. Vinyl and composite hold their shape in humidity, resist warping and fading, and wipe clean, which is why they go into bathrooms, kitchens, and any window that takes hours of direct sun. In a Coral Springs house that usually means a mix across the house, chosen room by room.

## Traditional tilt or hidden tilt

A traditional shutter has a center rod down each panel that you move to angle the louvers. A hidden tilt shutter moves them from a mechanism inside the panel, so the face stays clean and the spacing stays even. Hidden tilt suits newer construction; traditional suits a house with more period character. Both control light identically.

## Heat, not just light

A closed shutter puts a solid insulating layer between the glass and the room. On a west-facing Coral Springs room that is the difference between usable and avoided through the afternoon, and it takes measurable load off the air conditioning across a summer.

## Sliding doors and specialty shapes

Panel track shutters handle sliding glass doors: broad panels on a track that stack to one side. Arches, angles, and specialty shapes are templated on site and built to match, because a shutter is made to the opening either way.
