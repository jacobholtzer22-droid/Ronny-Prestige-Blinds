## Electric, motorized, remote-controlled: the same thing

The names get used interchangeably and they all describe one product: a blind or shade with a motor in the headrail instead of a cord. What changes between jobs is how it is powered and how you drive it, and those are the two decisions worth spending time on.

## Power: battery or hardwired

A rechargeable battery motor needs no wiring, which makes it the straightforward choice for a window that is awkward to open up, a finished room you do not want disturbed, or a retrofit. A hardwired motor runs off the electrical system and never needs charging, which suits new construction or a remodel where the walls are already open. Which one is right is decided by the opening rather than by preference, and we work that out on site.

## Control

A handheld remote, a wall switch, a phone app, or a smart home system with voice control. Most households end up using two: a switch by the door for daily use, the app when they are away. Through a smart home system you can put the shades on a schedule, which helps with heat in a west-facing room and makes an empty house look occupied.

## Which Coral Springs windows are worth it

The two-story entry windows in the newer sections of the city. Anything above a staircase. Skylights. The wide sliders onto the pool deck, where a cord is a nuisance every single day. Media rooms. The test we use is simple: if you would otherwise be reaching for a cord three or four times a day as the sun comes round, motorize it.

## Installation and programming

Our team fits the motor, sets the open and closed stop points, pairs the remote or switch, and connects it to your smart home system if you have one. You get a shade that works the way you expect before we leave, not a box and a manual.
